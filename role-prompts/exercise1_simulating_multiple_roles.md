## Objective: Understand how roles affect the model’s perspective.

    Write a prompt asking the same question (e.g., "What is climate change?").
- Prompt: "What is climate change?"
## Add role instructions for at least 3 personas, such as:

    Scientist → technical explanation

    Journalist → informative for the public

    Critic → skeptical tone
## Persona 1 — Scientist
  Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global and regional climate patterns attributable primarily to the increase in atmospheric greenhouse gas concentrations resulting from human activities since the pre-industrial period. The primary driver is the enhanced greenhouse effect, whereby gases such as carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O) absorb and re-emit longwave radiation, reducing the net outgoing energy flux from Earth's surface. Current atmospheric CO₂ concentration is approximately 422 ppm, representing a 50% increase over pre-industrial levels. Observed impacts include a global mean surface temperature increase of 1.1°C above the pre-industrial baseline, accelerated glacial retreat, sea level rise of 3.7 mm per year, and increased frequency of extreme weather events. The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (2021) concludes with high confidence that human influence is the dominant cause of observed warming since the mid-20th century.
## Persona 2 - Journalist
  Climate change refers to long-term shifts in global temperatures and weather patterns. While some of these changes occur naturally, scientists agree that human activities — particularly the burning of fossil fuels such as coal, oil, and gas — are the primary cause of the rapid changes observed since the mid-20th century.
When fossil fuels are burned, they release carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. These gases trap heat from the sun, causing the planet to warm. This process is commonly referred to as the greenhouse effect.
The effects of climate change are already being felt around the world. Average global temperatures have risen by approximately 1.1 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. Sea levels are rising, ice caps are melting, and extreme weather events such as floods, droughts, and wildfires are becoming more frequent and more severe.
Scientists warn that without significant reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, these impacts will worsen over time. International bodies such as the United Nations have called for urgent action to limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels.
## Persona 3 -  Critic
  Climate change is defined by mainstream scientific institutions as the long-term alteration of global temperature and precipitation patterns attributed primarily to anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions since the industrial period. The core physical mechanism — that CO₂ absorbs and re-emits longwave radiation — is well-established and not seriously disputed.
The areas warranting scrutiny are in the projection models and the policy responses derived from them.
On model uncertainty: Climate models produce a range of warming scenarios spanning 1.5°C to over 4°C by 2100. That is not a margin of error — it is a fourfold difference in outcome, each carrying dramatically different policy implications. The IPCC itself assigns probability ranges rather than point estimates, which is scientifically honest but rarely reflected in public communication or policy mandates.
On economic cost: Transition to net-zero by 2050 at the pace currently mandated by leading economies requires restructuring energy, transport, agriculture, and manufacturing simultaneously. The IMF estimates transition costs in the range of $3–6 trillion per year globally through 2050. The distribution of those costs — falling disproportionately on lower-income households and non-industrialised nations — is rarely central to the climate policy debate.
On proportionality: Acknowledging that warming is occurring and is human-influenced does not automatically validate any specific policy response. The appropriate policy question is not whether to act, but at what pace, at what cost, and with what burden-sharing mechanism. Those are empirical and political questions that the science alone cannot answer.
## Compare how the answers differ.
  All three personas are responding to the same question with access to the same underlying facts. What changes is not the information — it is:
  What gets foregrounded. The scientist leads with mechanism. The journalist leads with consequence. The critic leads with concession before pivoting to challenge.
  What gets omitted. The scientist never mentions cost. The journalist never mentions model uncertainty. The critic never mentions sea level data or attribution studies.
  Who the response is written for. Each persona has an assumed reader baked into the role instruction, and that assumed reader determines every stylistic choice that follows.
